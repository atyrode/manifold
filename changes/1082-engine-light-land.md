The landing concept's engine lights thin things from behind: grass blades and leaves let the sun or moon through, so a meadow against a low sun glows gold at its tips and a crown against the light glows at its rim, both shadowed where something stands between. Grass mixes dry and green blade by blade and flares where a gust bows it into the light. Tree crowns are shells of leaf clusters, each lit on its own side, with the sky showing through where their gaps meet; a birch's leaves hang in curtains, and in autumn every cluster turns its own colour in its species' range, an oak from russet to ochre, a birch from butter to lemon. Behind it, every kind now knows the cell's key light, the direction it was seen from and its distance from the eye, and reads the land under it from the core rather than from the ground (grass now fits its bounds to the land it stands on).
