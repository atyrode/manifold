The landing concept lab gains the first true 3D engine, shown by a new lab-only scene, Canvas: one world in metres seen through a free camera, with objects placed anywhere in it, a setup held as a document rather than in the address. Every cell casts one ray: the nearest object is lit by the World's sun or moon, shadowed by whatever stands in the way (the land's own hills included), lit by the sky and by any lamp nearby, and a mirror shows the world round it once; where nothing is hit, the World's sky shows in that direction, its clouds and stars included. Four kinds of object prove it: rolling ground with its relief, breadth and material (grass, earth, sand, snow, stone), blocks of plaster, timber, stone, brick or slate, spheres (matte, glossy or a mirror) and a lamp that lights the room it stands in. Three starter documents come with it: an eye standing in open land (Canvas opens on it), a room of stone walls with a window and a lit lamp, and a view straight down on spheres in the grass. Moving, turning or tuning an object only uploads its data; only a new kind, a larger count of a kind, or a mirror where there was none builds a new shader.
