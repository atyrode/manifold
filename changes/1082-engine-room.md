The landing concept's engine gains rooms and furniture as objects to place anywhere. A room is a floor, four walls and an optional ceiling, seen from inside and out: plaster in any tint, the lava lamp's papered rings, shoji, wood panelling or brick, over boards, tiles or carpet, with a skirting board and a coved cornice. Each wall holds up to three windows and a doorway or a closed door; the windows have frames and glazing bars in depth, a sill ledge inside and a stone sill outside, so the sun and the moon lay their patches through them onto the floor, the furniture and the walls. Window glass is the engine's first see-through surface: a pane shows the world outside, faintly tinted, with a slight reflection that shows the lit room at night, and it costs nothing in a setup without glass. A sideboard, a table and a wall shelf in any wood tone cast shadows and let things stand on them. A new starter, Lava room, rebuilds the lava lamp's room at midnight: papered walls, a teak sideboard with a lamp where the lava lamp stands, and the moon coming in through the windows to the left.
